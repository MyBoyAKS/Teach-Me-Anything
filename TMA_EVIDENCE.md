# TMA evidence: What the research says

Honest note first: **nobody has tested "Normal vs Widget vs Ultra" as packages.** What exists is strong research on the *ingredients* each mode uses. This page maps each ingredient to the evidence, with effect sizes where the papers report them.

How to read effect sizes: d or g ≈ 0.2 is small, ≈ 0.5 is medium, ≈ 0.8 is large. A g of 0.5 means the average learner with the technique scores higher than about 69% of learners without it.

---

## Ingredient by ingredient

| Ingredient | Used in | Evidence | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Worked examples** (step-by-step solved problems) | all modes | Barbieri et al. (2023), meta-analysis of 43 maths studies | **g = 0.48** (medium) |
| **Concrete first, then abstract** ("real numbers first, then letters") | all modes | Fyfe, McNeil, Son & Goldstone (2014), systematic review of *concreteness fading* in maths and science | consistent benefits for learning and transfer (review, no single pooled number) |
| **Animation instead of static pictures** (moving algebra, moving graph) | Widget, Ultra | Höffler & Leutner (2007), meta-analysis of 26 studies | **d = 0.37** overall; **d = 0.40** for representational animations |
| **Signaling / cueing** (highlight boxes, arrows, colour coding) | Widget, Ultra | Alpizar, Adesope & Wong (2020), meta-analysis | **d = 0.38** on learning outcomes |
| **Learner control of pace** (scrubbable timeline, pause, chapters) | Widget, Ultra | part of Mayer's *segmenting* principle (multimedia learning research) | positive on average; strongest for complex material |
| **Retrieval practice / quizzing** | Ultra (quiz, puzzles) | Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017), meta-analysis of 217 studies | testing beats restudying (about **g ≈ 0.5**), and beats doing nothing by much more |
| **Active / constructive / interactive engagement** (puzzles, explore lab, writing it down) | Ultra | Chi & Wylie (2014), *ICAP* framework | Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive, across many lab and classroom studies |

---

## What this suggests for each mode

| Mode | Strongest ingredients | Expected strength | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | worked examples, concrete first | **medium** (worked examples g ≈ 0.48) | mostly passive reading; works best when the learner then tries a similar problem |
| **Widget** | Normal's ingredients **plus** animation (d ≈ 0.37) and signaling (d ≈ 0.38) | **medium, with better understanding of *why*** | still mostly watching ("passive/active" in ICAP); too much animation can overload beginners, so keep it slow and one idea per step |
| **Ultra** | Widget's ingredients **plus** retrieval practice (g ≈ 0.5) and interactive tasks (top of ICAP) | **strongest, especially for remembering later and solving new problems** | takes the most time; the puzzles must be answered honestly (no peeking) to work |

Effects from different studies don't simply add up, so this is a ranking of what to expect, not a promise of a number.

**Another caveat (expertise reversal):** step-by-step guidance helps beginners most. As you get better, switch to Normal mode or straight to the Ultra quiz, because too much hand-holding can slow experts down.
